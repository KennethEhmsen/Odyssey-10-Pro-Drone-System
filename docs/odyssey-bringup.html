The four DShot assumptions 3 of 4 answered

These were listed in dependency order rather than cost order, and deliberately so — the one that could have invalidated the bidirectional design needed no instrument at all.

#AssumptionStatusEvidence

Assumption 1 was first because it was fatal, not because it was easy

“A TX and an RX channel can share one GPIO” had no fallback. If it had been false, bidirectional telemetry would have needed a different capture mechanism — not different settings — and the dynamic notch would have lost its measured input. It was confirmed on 2026-08-23 by reading a boot message, which cost nothing.

The largest untested block is the sensors

IMU:0 BACKUP:0 BARO:0 MAG:0 TOF:0 INA:0 — every driver in sensors.cpp is compiled but has never answered on a real bus. That is more unexercised firmware than DShot, the blackbox and the link layer combined, and it needs no instruments at all: just the parts, on the I²C bus.
