Three placements are load-bearing, not tidy-mindedness

The 120 mm mast is not decorative. The compass is what the return-to-home controller steers by, and it has to be far enough from the power train that hover currents do not bend it. Calibrate it with the motors spinning at hover throttle, not on a bench.
The IMU stack sits on polyurethane gel at exactly (0, 0, 0). Off the CG it reads translation as rotation; off the gel it reads the frame instead of the aircraft.
The downward ToF looks through a cut-out in the carbon. Anything hung under the belly — a gimbal, a bigger battery strap, a payload — blocks it, and a rangefinder reading an obstruction does not fail loudly. It reports a confident wrong altitude just as the aircraft decides whether it has landed.

What is drawn from what provenance

Derived, and reasoned, are not the same thing

Derived from config.h: the 387 mm wheelbase, the 9-inch propeller discs, and which corner each motor is in with which rotation — the plan view is drawn from those numbers, so it changes when the build switch changes.
Derived from the specification: every coordinate in the directory, from §8.1, and the mass groups from §3.1.
Not measured: none of this has been built. The masses are researched estimates that feed AIRFRAME_AUW_G, and §2 says plainly to weigh the parts as they arrive and correct them. A mass 50% out costs endurance and margin, not just money.
