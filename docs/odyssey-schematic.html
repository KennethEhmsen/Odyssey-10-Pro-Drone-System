UART allocation §9.1

PortPeripheralBaudPinsNote

Two peripherals on one port fails silently

AuxSerial and GnssSerial were both HardwareSerial(1). There is no compile error and no runtime error — the second begin() re-points the peripheral, and because sensors.cpp opens the GNSS after setup() opens the AUX bus, the AUX bus simply stopped existing. The beacon would have kept broadcasting a stale position and the Remote ID health line would never have gone HIGH. Finding 44; the AUX bus is now on UART0, which the USB console leaves free.

Power tree §9.5

The BEC gives 5 V and 12 V. It does not give 3.3 V — and every sensor here except the LiDAR wants 3.3 V, so the carrier has to make it. How much it must supply is a specification, not a detail.

Four rules that are not negotiable

1. The whole I²C bus is 3.3 V, including the pull-ups. GY-521 and GY-271 breakouts accept 5 V happily — and then drive 5 V into the P4 through the bus. One device on the wrong rail contaminates all six.
2. The SX1278 is 3.3 V only, power and every signal line. Nothing else is on SPI2, so nothing shifts levels for it either.
3. Anything on 5 V that drives a line into the P4 must output 3.3 V logic. The LiDAR, CRSF and GNSS all do; meter the VTX’s MSP TX before connecting it — that telemetry is optional, the pin is not.
4. The parachute servo takes its own pair from the BEC, never daisy-chained through the flight controller. A stalled 9 g servo pulls most of an amp, and the parachute fires exactly when the controller must stay alive.

What the carrier’s 3.3 V regulator must supply, beyond the P4 itself.
LoadContinuousPeakNote

Specify at least 300 mA of headroom, and decouple the radio

The ~270 mA peak assumes a LoRa transmit, an SD write and a ranging cycle land together — and nothing sequences them: the blackbox writes at 100 Hz, the LiDAR ranges continuously, telemetry transmits on its own schedule. 470 µF plus 100 nF at the SX1278’s own pins is required, not optional. A 120 mA transmit pulse on a shared rail is the classic MCU brown-out, and it presents as a random reset rather than as a power problem.
