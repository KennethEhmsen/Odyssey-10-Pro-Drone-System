What The Wire Should Look Like

Drawn to scale, for holding next to a capture. Every edge here comes from the same arithmetic the firmware uses, and the firmware has already printed its half of it on real hardware — 33 ticks/bit, 9999 ppm error, bidirectional.

One frame period 500 Hz · 2.6% duty

Almost all of it is idle. The burst is 52.8 µs out of 2000, and the ESC's reply lands in the gap just after it.

A single bit 33 ticks at 10 MHz

Bidirectional DShot is inverted, so the line idles HIGH and the pulse is LOW. A 1 is roughly twice as long as a 0, and that ratio is the whole discrimination — there is no clock.

Idling LOW is the failure that looks like a dead pin

Bidirectional DShot idles high specifically so the ESC can pull the line down to answer. Get the inversion wrong and the ESC ignores everything — which on a bench is indistinguishable from a pin that was never connected. Check the idle level before you check anything else.

The disarmed frame

This is the one you will actually capture first, and it has a silhouette you can recognise without decoding anything: twelve short pulses, then four long ones.

If you decode 48, stop

DSHOT_CMD_DISARM is value 0, and its frame is . DSHOT_MIN_THROTTLE is 48, and its frame is — a completely different shape.

If a disarmed aircraft is sending 48, idleMotors() is commanding minimum throttle rather than disarm, and the motors would turn on a disarmed aircraft. The two are far enough apart on the wire that you do not need a decoder to tell them apart, which is the point of putting both here.

The checksum is inverted, and only in bidirectional mode

crc = (x ^ (x>>4) ^ (x>>8)) & 0x0F, then inverted when bidirectional. An ESC configured for one mode rejects the other's frames on checksum — which is a loud failure rather than a quiet one, and is the right way round for a mode mismatch.

eRPM is electrical, and the pole count is not on the wire

The decoded period is electrical. Converting to a shaft rate needs the motor's pole count, and getting it wrong scales every derived frequency by a constant — which looks exactly like a mis-tuned notch rather than like a units error. That is why the tracker prefers a measurement but still gates it on the four motors agreeing: a systematic scale error would agree with itself.
