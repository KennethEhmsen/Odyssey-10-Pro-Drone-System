The decoder reads old logs on purpose

v2 and v3 records still decode. A flight recorder whose reader only understands the newest format silently makes older flights unreadable, which is the opposite of what a flight recorder is for — so every version is appended at the end and nothing is ever reordered or resized.

The header carries recordBytes, and the decoder checks it against the size it computes for the declared version. A log written by a build whose struct drifted is rejected at the door rather than silently misread by a fixed offset.

Why the verdict is logged and not the spectrum

The record carries the tracker’s conclusion — centre frequency, confidence, and these flags — never the spectrum it came from. That is forced, not preferred: the log runs at 100 Hz, so its Nyquist limit is 50 Hz, and every notch frequency in the build matrix is 88–180 Hz. A 120 Hz motor peak recorded at 100 Hz does not appear at 120 Hz — it aliases down to 20 Hz, and no post-processing recovers it.

So the spectrum is analysed on board at the full loop rate, where the peak is genuinely visible, and only the scalar is written down. ODY_NOTCH_FLAG_MEASURED is the one to read first: it says whether the centre came from RPM telemetry or from the tracker’s own search.
